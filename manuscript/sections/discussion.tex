\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{Temporal ordering drives the representation gap.}
The C-index difference between Setting 4 (decoupled age--event encoding,
0.6233) and Setting 3 (prose serialisation, 0.6156), using identical
Qwen3-8B encoders and raw features, is the cleanest signal in our
results.
Prose serialisation collapses temporal ordering into semantic proximity:
the sentence \textit{``At age 42.0, J45''} and \textit{``At age 52.1, F32''}
differ only in surface tokens, but the 8-year interval between them is
invisible to the encoder's attention mechanism.
Decoupled encoding preserves this structure explicitly: the 128-dim
sinusoidal age vector ensures that events at age 42 and age 52.1 are
geometrically separated before the token embedding is appended.
This is consistent with the view that surface semantic encoding loses
prognostically relevant temporal structure when event ordering drives
the outcome.
The +0.008 C-index advantage of S4 over S3 is the primary empirical claim;
bootstrap confidence intervals confirm this gap exceeds sampling noise:
CI\,=\,($+$0.005, $+$0.010), $p<0.001$.
Setting 2 (Delphi's ordinal token stream) falls between S3 and S4:
temporal ordering is preserved as positional structure in the input
sequence, but the autoregressive hazard chain cannot separate
ordering effects from pre-training priors (see calibration gap below).

\paragraph{Setting~3-Full: LLMs encode numeric biomarker structure from prose.}
Setting~3-Full (full-text prose, no raw features) achieves C\,=\,0.6195,
matching the 39-feature CoxPH baseline (0.6191) while using no raw
numeric features.
This suggests that Qwen3-8B's pre-training has internalised enough
biomarker-range semantics to distinguish, e.g., HbA1c\,=\,52 from
HbA1c\,=\,38 in terms of mortality relevance.
The TD-AUC advantage at the earliest evaluated horizon (0.605 vs.\ 0.584 at 9.5\,yr) further
suggests the embedding captures interactions between biomarker values and
disease history that a linear CoxPH on raw features misses.
However, this encoding is \emph{implicit} and cannot be inspected or
enforced, which limits trustworthiness in high-stakes clinical deployment.

\paragraph{Encoder and fusion ablations.}
The encoder and fusion ablations confirm that the encoding-format findings are
robust rather than artefacts of a specific implementation choice.
For encoder choice, BCB and Qwen3-8B produce near-identical C-indices for S3
and S4, confirming that discriminative information is accessible to both
encoder families; the temporal-ordering advantage of S4 over S3 persists
($\Delta$C\,=\,$+$0.008 for Qwen, $p<0.001$).
The anomalously high S3-Full·BCB C-index (0.6371) is explained by 512-token
truncation retaining primarily age signal, which collapses TD-AUC to 0.507.
For fusion strategy, concatenation outperforms summation by
$+$0.028--$+$0.039 C-index across all cells, confirming that retaining
independent embedding dimensions alongside raw features preserves more
representational capacity than projective fusion.
The primary conclusion, that serialisation format drives discrimination more
than encoder family or fusion method, is supported across both ablations.

\paragraph{Calibration gap in zero-shot deployment.}
In our zero-shot implementation, Delphi exhibits a calibration gap
(IBS\,0.185 vs.\ $\approx$0.141); this should not be interpreted as a
definitive limitation of autoregressive EHR transformers, because Delphi
receives only ICD event streams without HbA1c, CRP, creatinine, or
cystatin~C --- the gap captures missing acute biomarker signal as much as a
paradigm deficiency.
The AUC collapse at the earliest evaluated horizon (0.52 at 9.5\,yr) is
consistent with this: short-horizon mortality is dominated by physiological
derangements absent from the ICD stream under distribution shift from
pre-training.
Fine-tuning with biomarker inputs and a calibration-aware objective would
likely close this gap, at the cost of zero-shot generalisability.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
All embedding methods freeze their encoders; fine-tuning may yield
additional gains at substantially higher compute cost.
IBS and TD-AUC rely on independent censoring assumptions that may be
violated in UKB.
All results are from a single biobank cohort; external validation is
required before clinical translation.
Bio\_ClinicalBERT's 512-token limit is an architectural constraint that
disproportionately affects Setting~3-Full; future work should evaluate
longer-context clinical encoders.
The primary evaluation metrics (C-index, TD-AUC) measure discrimination,
while IBS provides calibration context; patient-interpretable life-year
estimates (RMST) and interaction with stronger survival heads are left to
future work.
